Types of Trust

The Trusts we use most often

Protective Property Trust (PPT)

Standard advice for couples who own their home together, and very cost-effective.

You own the home as “tenants in common”, each with a share. Your share goes into Trust in your Will: the survivor can live there for life, and your share is guaranteed to reach your chosen beneficiaries.

Protects against

  • The whole home going on care fees
  • “Sideways disinheritance” if the survivor remarries
  • A beneficiary's divorce or debts

Family Protection Trust (FPT)

A separate document set up now, not only after death. Works for sole owners, and can hold investments too (with separate financial advice).

It protects the next generation. It isn't a way to avoid care fees.

Can help with

  • No probate on a mortgage-free home
  • Can't be challenged after six years
  • Protection from a beneficiary's divorce or debt
  • Inheritance Tax for future generations

Life Interest & Right to Occupy

Let someone live in your home, rent-free, while guaranteeing it passes to your chosen people afterwards. Ideal for second marriages and blended families.

A Life Interest lasts for life; a Right to Occupy ends on events you choose.

Good for

  • A new partner living in your home
  • Children from a previous relationship
  • Adult children still living at home
  • Cash and investments too

Discretionary Trust

Everything goes into one “pot”. Your Trustees decide who gets what, and when, guided by your confidential Letter of Wishes.

Because no one is entitled to a set amount, their share can't be counted against them.

Good for a beneficiary who…

  • Is divorcing, or in debt
  • Isn't good with money
  • Has an addiction or learning difficulty
  • Relies on means-tested benefits (savings cap currently £16,000)
