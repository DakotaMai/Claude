If you died without a Will, who would inherit?

Without a valid Will, a “pecking order” set by law decides who gets what, and you get no say. Answer a few quick questions to find out what would happen to your estate. It takes 30 seconds, and nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere.

For deaths in England & Wales on or after 26 July 2023. This is general information, not advice. Every family is different, so please talk to us about your situation.

Good to know

Things the rules don't tell you

{{icon:heart}}

Unmarried partners get nothing

However long you've lived together, there's no automatic right to inherit. The same applies to stepchildren, unless legally adopted.

{{icon:home}}

Jointly owned assets

Anything owned as “joint tenants”, such as a home or bank account, passes straight to the surviving joint owner, outside the rules.

{{icon:users}}

No executors

With no Will there are no named executors, so a family member has to apply to administer the estate. That can be slower, costlier and more stressful.

{{icon:clock}}

Children inherit at 18

Including adopted children. If a child has died, their own children usually step into their place.

{{icon:scale}}

The law keeps changing

The rules changed in 2009, 2014, 2020 and 2023. Even if they match your wishes today, they might not tomorrow.

{{icon:shield}}

No protection

No Trusts, no guardians for your children and no protection against care fees, divorce or debt. A Will puts you back in control.
