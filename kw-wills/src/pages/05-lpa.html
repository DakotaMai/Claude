Lasting Powers of Attorney

A Will protects your family after you've gone. An LPA protects you while you're alive.

If you lose the ability to manage your own affairs, no one can automatically step in, not even your spouse. An LPA lets you choose who can.

Three types of LPA

Choose the protection you need

Property & Financial LPA

Lets someone you trust deal with your money and property. Not only for loss of capacity: it also helps if you're housebound, hard of hearing, or can't sign your name.

Your Attorneys can

  • Pay your bills
  • Deal with your bank
  • Talk to companies on the phone
  • Sell a property for you

Health & Welfare LPA

Used only after you lose mental capacity. Without one, health professionals and the local authority decide what's in your best interests, which may not be what you or your family would want.

Your Attorneys can

  • Challenge NHS and local authority decisions
  • Refuse or request treatment, including resuscitation
  • Liaise with social services about where you live
  • Discuss every aspect of your care

Business LPA

A separate LPA for your business, with different Attorneys from your personal affairs if you wish. Advised even for sole traders.

Your Attorneys can

  • Pay suppliers and staff
  • Deal with the bank
  • Sign documents for you
  • Sell or lease business premises
