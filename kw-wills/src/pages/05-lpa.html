Lasting Powers of Attorney

A Will protects your family after you've gone. An LPA protects you while you're alive.

If accident, illness or age ever leaves you unable to manage your own affairs, no one can automatically step in to help. Not even your husband, wife or children. An LPA lets you choose the people you trust.

Three types of LPA

Choose the protection you need

Most people have both personal LPAs. Business owners can add a third.

Property & Financial LPA

Lets a trusted friend or family member deal with your money and property, and you can place restrictions on what they can and can't do. Your attorneys can:

  • Pay your bills
  • Deal with your bank
  • Talk to companies on the phone
  • Sell a property for you

It isn't only for loss of mental capacity. Sometimes your mind is absolutely fine but you still need a hand. You can use it with your consent if:

  • You're housebound and can't get to the bank
  • You're hard of hearing and struggle with phone calls
  • Arthritis or an injury makes it difficult to sign your name

For couples especially

  • If one of you loses capacity, the other can't sell your joint home to release capital
  • Joint accounts can be frozen
  • Without authority to act for your partner, conflicts of interest can arise
  • The Court of Protection may have to step in, adding cost and delay

Health & Welfare LPA

Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005, no one, including a spouse, can make decisions for you if you lose capacity. Health professionals and the local authority must then decide what they believe is in your best interests, and that may not be what you or your family would choose.

A person is judged to lack capacity when they can no longer understand the information needed to make a decision, weigh it up, retain it for long enough, or communicate the decision.

This LPA can only be used after you lose capacity. Your attorneys can:

  • Challenge decisions made by the NHS and local authority
  • Refuse or request medication and treatment
  • Liaise with social services about where you live, including keeping you at home if your family can care for you
  • Make sure family and friends are involved, and your wishes respected
  • Discuss every aspect of your care, including daily routine, diet and dress

Decisions that really matter

  • Whether you'd want to be resuscitated
  • Respecting religious beliefs, for example about blood transfusions
  • Pursuing a specialist or experimental treatment your GP may not know about
  • Ongoing decisions if your health is likely to deteriorate

Business LPA

If you own a business or have business partners, a separate Property & Financial LPA for commercial matters lets you appoint different attorneys for your business than for your personal affairs, so the business keeps running if you can't. It's advised even for sole traders.

Your business attorneys oversee the business and can delegate day-to-day running to people best placed to do it. They can:

  • Pay suppliers and staff
  • Deal with the bank
  • Talk to companies on the phone
  • Sign documents for you
  • Sell or lease business premises

Why keep it separate?

The people you'd trust with your family finances aren't always the people best placed to run your business. A separate LPA keeps the two apart, avoids conflicts of interest and helps prevent financial and legal difficulties for the business, your staff and your partners.

Practicalities

Registration and timing

When should I set up an LPA?

While you're well. If LPAs are needed and not already in place, it's too late: you must have capacity to make one. Many people find an LPA is as important as their Will, if not more so.

Does an LPA need to be registered?

Yes. An LPA can only be used once it's registered with the Office of the Public Guardian (OPG), which charges a fee per document. Depending on your circumstances, the fee may be halved or waived. We'll tell you the current fee at your appointment.

Should I register straight away?

You can register immediately or at any point later. Registration should take a few weeks, but it has taken six months or more at busy times. If your LPA is unregistered and suddenly needed, your family could be left unable to act until it's registered, so we usually recommend registering promptly.

Who should I choose as my attorneys?

People you trust completely, such as a spouse or partner, adult children or close friends. You can appoint more than one, decide whether they must act together, add replacements, and include restrictions or guidance. Sevvy will talk you through the options.
